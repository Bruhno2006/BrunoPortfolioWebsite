***Originally written in REELHOUSE***

Acting as a career is a taxing process that requires skill, range, and experience for actors to properly “get into character”, expressing raw emotion all while confined to a fictional reality. One can describe it as “living truthfully under imaginary circumstances”.

So, it’s understandable that actors usually focus on developing and portraying one role in a film but some dare to handle… **multiple!?**

Allow me to take you through a list of actors who have played multiple characters in one film and explore what methods and techniques were incorporated to provide the illusion of “seeing double”.

# Live-Action Actors

## Robert Pattinson (*Mickey 17*)

Handling clone characters results in some difficulties and production challenges. Lookalike characters need to interact naturally and, if they’re written to be distinct, there needs to be ways to differentiate the characters from one another. In Bong Joon Ho’s *Mickey 17*, the director considered this for Mickey Barnes, played by Robert Pattinson.

Mickey works as an “Expendable”, a disposable crew member tasked to constant suicide missions and experiments after each death and reprint, to benefit his Trumpian bosses’ space colonisation project. After enduring this cycle 17 times, there came the problem of two reprinted Mickey Barnes variants, referred to as Mickey 17 and 18 respectively, coexisting together in a world where multiples are deemed illegal. 

![Mickey 17](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/d9a0437b985887fd7110dc6a7d45366d19135f8a/214_0_5696_3419/master/5696.jpg?width=700&quality=85&auto=format&fit=max&s=88e02062091e1cad903d7bdb7d3e2781 "Mickey 17")

For the film, Pattinson needed to subtly portray the replicated characters as separate people with different mannerisms, personalities, and ideals. So for Mickey 17, the actor made him more cautious, cowardly, and soft-spoken. Mickey 18, however, is more aggressive, impulsive, and consistently defiant towards his higher-ups.

As a result, viewers were able to differentiate the Mickeys as their own characters through Pattinson's performance. Not surprising really; after escaping the edgy teenager image of *Twilight*, Pattinson has gained lots of experience taking a wider variety of roles, such as *The Batman (2022)*, *Primetime*, *Dune*, and even voice-acting in Studio Ghibli's *The Boy and the Heron*.

## Michael B. Jordan (*Sinners*)

The approach used in *Mickey 17* can be expanded to other aspects of a character. This is showcased in Ryan Coogler's *Sinners*, as the director develops the narrative roles of the Moore twins, Smoke and Stack, played by Michael B. and Michael C. Jordan (I know, a very overused joke).

Set in 1932, the twins return to Mississippi to open a juke joint, unaware of a sudden vampire invasion. Thanks to the help of dialect coach Beth McGuire, Jordan was able to get distinct voices, postures, and physical mannerisms for Smoke and Stack.

![Sinners](https://miro.medium.com/v2/1*jVZbKVn0hm7el9GYW37XrA.jpeg "Sinners")

However, Coogler also wanted to show the twins’ differences through their clothing; Ruth E. Carter, the costume designer, explains that while subtle, the Moore Twins’ costumes are substantial in telling their story. Smoke wears blue and rugged clothing; Stack wears red and is more smartly dressed than his brother.

These decisions resulted in emotionally grounded and visually distinct characters in the film. Jordan went on to receive Best Actor in a Leading Role at the 2025 *Oscars* - his first-ever Academy Award. Very much deserved!

Both *Mickey 17* and *Sinners* utilised a body double and VFX technology to create a seamless dual performance. While *Mickey 17* relied on Pattinson playing both Mickeys consecutively, *Sinners* used a motion-controlled camera that repeats the camera’s movement and speed across multiple passes, allowing Jordan’s separate performances (as Smoke and Stack respectively) to be stitched together in post-production.

# Voice Actors in Animation

Though I have only discussed live-action actors, let’s not forget those in animation. I believe portraying characters is harder via voice acting, as actors’ performances depend solely on their vocal ability. Yet even with these limitations, voice actors have proven, in multiple instances, that they’re on par with other Hollywood actors in films, animated or not.

I'd refer to their ability as ***literal throat magic*** (pause).

## Seth MacFarlane

![Family Guy](https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/familyguy/images/c/cf/Dacx20_640_02-0139_hires2.jpg/revision/latest?cb=20161017232644 "Family Guy")

Actor and animator Seth MacFarlane is perhaps the best example of a voice actor with a wide range. He has voiced characters in animated television shows including, *Family Guy*, *The Cleveland Show*, *American Dad*, and the upcoming *Ted: The Animated Series*. All of MacFarlane’s characters are so distinct from one another that it’s hard to believe that all their back-and-forth banter is performed by one person.

MacFarlane stated on T*he Graham Norton Show* that he got inspired by actors from live-action movies/shows while using certain voice acting techniques - Stewie Griffin was modelled on the upper-class British accent of Rex Harrison; no wonder he says “cool whip” weirdly.

## Eric Bauza

![Coyote vs. ACME](https://www.animationscoop.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Bugs-2-Coyote-Acme-900.jpg "Coyote vs. ACME")

Another prominent voice actor is Eric Bauza, most notable for voicing most of the *Looney Tunes* cast, including Bugs Bunny and Daffy Duck. His performance in the cartoons acts as an ode to Mel Blanc, “The Man of a Thousand Voices”, and his previous work with the *Looney Tunes* characters from as early as the 1940s. Each character has a unique voice, as actors would change their pitch or include an accent or speech impediments to fit their personality in the cartoon.

For Bugs, the voice actor would do a smooth, fast-talking hybrid of a Brooklyn and Bronx accent, talking as if he’s a calm, sarcastic trickster. For Daffy meanwhile, the voice would include a lisp, and act more erratic and self-absorbed.

Bauza’s work for *Looney Tunes* cartoons and movies, including T*he Day the Earth Blew Up* and *Coyote vs. Acme*, and his wide experience in cartoons outside of the IP are what made him so well known today.

***As of writing, Eric Bauza had passed away October 25th 2026. May he rest in peace.***

# Conclusion

As stated before, acting in any form is a difficult skill, so it’s impressive to see actors challenging themselves as well as production’s creativity in implementing the “seeing double” illusion while having it be integral to the story.

I hope to see more of this dedication present in the newest batch of movies and TV shows. But in the meantime, here are some honourable mentions and recommendations: 

#### Jordan Peele's *Us*

![Us](https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcSn5cFNEfbv7CRAfILUr2olo8C70AI_HstP-DK94_xClx75CcJLprhXUnA&s=10 "Us")

A family fights to survive against mysterious, empty shadow lookalikes of each member. This includes the scuffle between Adelaide Wilson and Red, both played by Lupita Nyong’o.

#### *The Sympathizer*

![The Sympathizer](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/9cdd48bf0c6cdb5fea29ad6bb1b9e2811474e768/0_76_1500_900/master/1500.jpg?width=1200&height=900&quality=85&auto=format&fit=crop&s=6426dd37118c9431e89439d8b171cf46 "The Sympathizer")

This limited series follows a Vietnam spy who takes refuge in Los Angeles, where he has to gather intelligence and report back to the Viet Cong. Robert Downey Jr. stars in this, playing four characters, each symbolising pillars of the overwhelming white American patriarchy. 

#### M. Night Shyamalan's *Split*

![Split](https://cdn.i-scmp.com/sites/default/files/styles/1020x680/public/images/methode/2017/01/16/23420544-d967-11e6-bbb7-6a43a6f882fe_1280x720.jpg?itok=WJr0WdPW "Split")

James McAvoy plays Kevin, an individual with dissociative identity disorder. McAvoy portrays 23 separate personas - some friendly, others hostile, and one absolutely dangerous. 3 kidnapped teenagers plan on escaping and avoiding his various personas.

#### Wes Craven's *Scream*

![Scream](https://www.denofgeek.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/scr14979r.jpg?resize=768%2C432 "Scream")

I know I'm shifting the goalpost, but in this slasher horror, the Ghostface identity is donned by multiple perpetrators as they go on a murderous spree on high school students. This subverts traditionally tropes in slasher films and created more psychological tension, as they could generally be anyone - even side characters!

#### Joe and Anthony Russo (The Russo Brothers)'s *Avengers: Doomsday*

![Avengers: Doomsday](https://cosmicbook.news/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/avengers-doomsday-everything-we-know.webp "Avengers: Doomsday")

Another Robert Downey Jr. mention! After his 10-year run as Iron Man, Downey Jr. returns in the next upcoming *Avengers* film to play Doctor Doom. The Avengers, the Fantastic Four, the Thunderbolts, and the previous X-Men band together to stop him and save the multiverse.
