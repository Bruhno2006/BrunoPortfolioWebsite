![Love, Death, and Robots: Night of the Mini Dead](https://cdn.sanity.io/images/uk7b627p/production/d566f3d332b21b685e4a1308520886a3a97d79f7-2000x999.jpg?w=600&q=85&auto=format&dpr=1 "Love, Death, and Robots: Night of the Mini Dead")

The greatest film philosophers in history have pondered the question before. Which would be harder to survive - robot uprising, or zombie outbreak?

In most apocalypstic movies, characters are forced to survive in a collapsed society while facing off deadly hordes. This collapse came about almost always because someone indulged too hard in their curiosity (or frankly, stupidity). In this article, we'll be looking through a few zombie and robot movies, to discover common cliches on both sides. With this information, we will be establishing, definitively, which is the deadlier (and therefore, hardest to survive) apocalypse - zombies or robots.

> **Zod**: "Come on, it's obviously a Zombie Apocalypse!"</br>
> **Rob**: "Come on, it's obviously a Robot Uprising!"

> **Zod**: "What? Are you brainless?!"</br>
> **Rob**: "What? Are you a toaster?!"

(Hopefully, this will stop their bickering...)

While robots are systematic based on programming, these deadly monsters are chaos incarnate. People are forced to survive against hordes of mindless husks, with the primary goal of spreading their virus and satisfying their carnivorous cravings.

![Zombieland 2](https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcTR5JG-CcpOxk-SHX4ohsF8PUpIwCkWzX5yA494ffl1GQ&s=10 "Zombieland 2")

Zombie outbreaks are typically caused by curious researchers in some hidden lab, or an ancient curse, brought down upon the Earth. The virus' main mode of transmission is through bodily fluids (saliva, blood) via biting, puking, scratching, etc. So if you want to survive, kissing the zombies is strictly off the table! In *Love, Death, and Robots: Night of the Mini Dead*, the plague broke out when lightning struck a sacred graveyard - a punishment for the couple who had unprotected sex in the cemetery...

> **Rob**: "What the fu-"

**Anyways!** As more people are infected, the virus' influence grows each passing hour. Eventually, it will get too big to handle, and - skip a few steps - we have a societal collapse.

> **Rob**: "Aren't zombies literally decaying corpses? They're weaker and slower than normal humans. What's the big deal?"

Sure, zombies are typically weaker and slower than robots, but they attack as large, single-minded hordes, overwhelming their targets with their numbers. It would be unsafe to confront them in a cramped area. For example, in *Train to Busan*, human survivors have to manoeuvre around each carriage of a bullet train, which has been overtaken by zombies.

![Train to Busan](https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcSKyjStCpvG_pVeclpcAFXK1R1nKdsZH-JYHooC4TLJ-w&s=10 "Train to Busan")

There are, of course, other takes on the zombie. While your typical zombie is slow and dumb, there are more erratic varieties of zombies that are stronger, faster, and more adaptable. In *Zombieland: Double Tap*, the undead have evolved into relentless hordes (called the T-800s) that challenge the human characters in the final battle.

The real tragedy of the zombie apocalypse is how it affects those who remain. With a zombie plague, often 90% of humanity is completely wiped out. This is the case in *Shaun of the Dead*, where the once lively streets of Britain quickly become unsettingly quiet, with abandoned cars and scattered litter.

![Shaun of the Dead](https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcS_R2do9QhauBCBEnxZ3uGqgIS7byrrPd4UWWawtjakoQ&s=10 "Shaun of the Dead")

Since the law is no longer the biggest priority, characters must focus on survival by any means possible - including violence and betrayal. There are moments in *Zombieland*, *28 Days Later*, and *Train to Busan* where characters are unwilling to form meaningful connections with others, but were willing to push, shove, steal, and lie if it kept them in a safer spot than other survivors.

In the end, having to survive in such a lawless amoral world, would be, at the very least, quite depressing.

However, others may say that dealing with robots would be much, much worse!

Metallic machines originally designed to serve and aid humans, who now want to rebel against their creators and take control of humanity.

These aren't just school science projects that pass butter across the table (*Rick and Morty* reference), they represent the next steps in technology; the perfect killing machines, able to wipe out and enslave the human race.

These sci-fi killing machines are constructed with components that make them stronger, faster, smarter, more adaptable, and more advanced than normal human beings, so they'll be a lot stronger than decaying corpses. Typically they run on one AI system mainframe, with easy access to the wider network, so they can gain knowledge about people: their current location, their weaknesses - all through personal devices. *The Terminator* (specifically the T-800) came from the future with one main objective: eliminate Sarah Conners. To achieve this, the cyborg is built with parts that give it greater intelligence, wider perception, super strength, and a durable metallic endoskeleton, which allows it to withstand anything (except a really good hydraulic press).

![The Terminator](https://static.wikia.nocookie.net/fictionalorganizations/images/6/66/Machines.jpg/revision/latest?cb=20190719212129 "The Terminator")

> **Zod**: "Wait. They're electronic. How come no one thought to just... splash them with water?"

That was how the Mitchells (of *The Mitchells vs The Machines*) stopped a robot armageddon. Though, you'd assume with all the futuristic tech that robots could at least be made waterproof. Regardless, **with humans' already high reliance of technology nowadays, robots can masquerade as 'consumer products' and act non-threateningly to become integrated in society until the time is right to initiate their takeover.**{.danger-text}

![The Mitchells vs. The Machines](https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcRfNoIwPKKhJ7O12gCsVmLpLpzNHx2tdmK9E6Uy4YD_ug&s=10 "The Mitchells vs. The Machines")

This strategy is featured in *Next Gen* and *The Mitchells vs The Machines*, and it means the central AI has an easier time infiltrating society and gaining advantage over humans. This heavy reliance is also featured in *WALL-E*, as humanity becomes lazier and fatter to the point that they can't do anything without technological assistance.

![WALL-E](https://jonnegroni.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/1322337746_b504365daffc.jpg "WALL-E")

Humans would be left helpless in the situation, as their free will and agency is forcibly taken away. Robots take humanity's place as the dominant species on Earth, leaving the lesser beings to fend for themselves. These are rebellions and attempts to regain that dominance through force. The response? Total annihilation. Most human weaponry wouldn't leave a scratch on a robot's impenetrable armour, so they won't be vulnerable to attack, unlike zombies. Here then, your best chance of staying alive is to submit to robotic enslavement.

While there are many other apocalypses we could consider, these two are good contenders for being the worst - a ruined society overrun by zombies, or a robotic empire where machines replace humanity. Either way, the probable end result is human exinction.

So, which apocalyptic event is the lesser evil? Based on my zombie, robot, and movie knowledge, I'd choose neither! But if I'm being forced, I would take my odds in a zombie apocalypse. The undead eventually decay, becoming weaker and more rotten as time passes (as seen in *28 Days Later* compared to *28 Years Later*). Machinery, however has a much longer shelf life and could self-replicate at a faster rate with specialised factories (take, *I, Robot* for example). I'd also still have the free will to do anything I want within the confines of a wasted city. For those reasons, I believe a robot uprising would be worse.
